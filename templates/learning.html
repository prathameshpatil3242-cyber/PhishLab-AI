Why these examples matter

Reading real phishing messages is uncomfortable and risky. The scenarios used here are invented for classroom use: they copy the patterns attackers use (pressure, fear, reward, fake verification) without copying any real campaign or organisation.

Remember the three checks

  • Who really sent it? (look at the address after @)
  • What is it asking you to do? (password, money, attachment?)
  • Where does the link actually go? (read the registered domain)
